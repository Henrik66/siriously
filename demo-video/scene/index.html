How a 4-billion-parameter model fits in under a gigabyte

8 min read · November 2026

Ternary models store every weight as −1, 0 or +1. That is about 1.6 bits per weight instead of 16, so a 4B model shrinks from 8 GB to under 1 GB and runs comfortably on a laptop.

Because multiplying by −1, 0 or +1 is just adding, subtracting or skipping, inference is mostly memory-bound. Apple silicon's unified memory makes that fast: no copies between CPU and GPU.

The catch is training. Ternary weights have to be learned that way from the start; squeezing a finished model afterwards loses too much accuracy.
